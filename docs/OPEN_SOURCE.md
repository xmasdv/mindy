# Open-Source Model

Mindy's core is open-source software under the [Mozilla Public License 2.0](../LICENSE). Commercial use is permitted, subject to the license terms.

> Mindy is at an early, pre-implementation stage. This repository currently contains product, architecture, security, roadmap, and governance documents; it does not contain a runnable client or build.

## Core licensing

- New contributions to this repository are submitted under MPL-2.0 unless a path explicitly identifies another compatible license.
- Thunderbird-derived files already covered by the MPL remain subject to their applicable MPL requirements, including preservation of notices and source availability obligations when distributed.
- The MPL applies at the file level. Read the [official license text](../LICENSE) before distributing covered source or executables.
- No Contributor License Agreement (CLA) currently exists.

Relicensing third-party contributions would require adequate rights from contributors and a separate, explicit governance decision. Mindy makes no promise that the core or any contribution will be relicensed.

## Sustainable development

Open source does not require every future Mindy offering or asset to use the MPL. Future services, hosted features, models, themes, signing or update services, support, and separately distributed assets may use other terms when they are technically and legally separated and their licensing is explicit.

That separation cannot remove MPL obligations from covered core or Thunderbird-derived files. Commercial offerings must not imply that contributors, Mozilla, Thunderbird, or other third parties provide their warranty, support, or endorsement.

## Contribution model

Issues and thoughtful proposals are welcome during the current design phase. Code pull requests should wait until there is a scoped issue, accepted design, task-level plan, and upstream scaffold; documentation and governance improvements may be proposed now.

Contributors should:

1. Read [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md).
2. Keep issue titles, descriptions, and technical artifacts in English where practical.
3. Preserve upstream license notices and identify copied or adapted material.
4. Participate respectfully and keep proposals within the project's stated scope.

This document explains the project's intended model; it is not legal advice.
